## Overview

Costco operates membership warehouses and e-commerce sites based on the concept of offering low prices on a limited selection of nationally-branded and private-label products to achieve high sales volumes and rapid inventory turnover [10-K line 00134]. The company buys most merchandise directly from suppliers, routes it through cross-docking consolidation points or directly to warehouses, creating freight volume and handling efficiencies that lower costs compared to traditional distribution channels [10-K line 00135]. Costco's warehouse format features a no-frills, self-service layout with strict inventory controls, including membership requirements, to reduce shrinkage and labor costs [10-K line 00139]. The company operates in multiple countries and offers a range of categories including foods and sundries, non-foods, fresh foods, warehouse ancillary (such as gasoline), and other businesses like e-commerce and travel [10-K line 00143]. Membership fees are $65 annually in the U.S., with Executive memberships offering additional rewards redeemable at warehouses [10-K line 00157].

## Market Positioning and Growth Outlook



Costco holds a strong competitive position supported by specific advantages. The company's low-price strategy combined with high-quality merchandise and efficient distribution helps differentiate it from competitors like Walmart, Target, Amazon, and other warehouse clubs [10-K line 00190]. Its private-label brand, Kirkland Signature, is noted for being high quality and offered at prices generally lower than national brands, contributing to higher margins and cost reduction [10-K line 00192]. Additionally, the company's membership format provides a loyal customer base and recurring revenue that supports profitability and sustainability [10-K line 00406].

Costco faces several risks including competitive pressures from global, national, and regional wholesalers and retailers, including those with e-commerce operations [10-K line 00190]. Currency fluctuations affect net sales and gross margins, especially in international operations [10-K line 00409]. Rising costs related to employee compensation, healthcare, and utilities are partially outside the company’s control [10-K line 00407]. Additionally, government actions such as tariffs can increase merchandise costs [10-K line 00403].
