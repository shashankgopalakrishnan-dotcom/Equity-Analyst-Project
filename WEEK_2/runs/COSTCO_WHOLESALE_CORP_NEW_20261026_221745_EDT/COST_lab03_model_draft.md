# Overview

Costco operates a membership warehouse business model focused on offering low prices on a limited selection of nationally-branded and private-label products [134]. This approach allows for high sales volumes and rapid inventory turnover, enabling profitability at significantly lower gross margins compared to most retailers [134]. The company buys merchandise directly from suppliers, utilizing cross-docking consolidation points or direct shipping to warehouses, which enhances freight volume and handling efficiencies [135]. With an average warehouse space of approximately 148,000 square feet, Costco’s layout supports efficient selling space, merchandise handling, and inventory control [139]. The membership format, including exclusive shopping hours and strict entrance/exit controls, minimizes inventory shrinkage compared to typical retail operations [139].

# Market Positioning and Growth Outlook

Costco competes globally with a wide range of wholesalers and retailers such as supermarkets, supercenters, online retailers, gasoline stations, hard discounters, department stores, and specialty stores [190]. In the U.S., its main competitors include Walmart, Target, Kroger, and Amazon, along with other warehouse clubs like Sam’s Club and BJ’s Wholesale Club [190]. Key competitive advantages include its membership model, low prices, broad selection of high-quality merchandise, and efficient distribution systems [134]. The company also prioritizes member satisfaction through return policies, technical support, and extended warranties on certain items [142].

Key catalysts for growth include membership growth and retention—reporting a 92.3% renewal rate in the U.S. and Canada and an 89.8% worldwide [167]—successful market expansion, supply chain efficiency, continued development of its private-label Kirkland Signature brand [192], and digital transformation efforts that contributed to 11% of total net sales being from e-commerce in 2026 [153].
