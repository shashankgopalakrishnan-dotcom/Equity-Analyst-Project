# Overview
Costco Wholesale Corp. operates membership warehouses and e-commerce sites based on a business model that emphasizes offering low prices on a limited selection of nationally-branded and private-label products [10-K line 00134]. The company leverages high sales volumes and rapid inventory turnover through volume purchasing, efficient distribution, and reduced handling in no-frills, self-service warehouse facilities [10-K line 00134]. Its strategy focuses on providing members with a broad range of high-quality merchandise at consistently lower prices than elsewhere [10-K line 00141]. Membership fees are $65 annually in the U.S., varying by country, and include a free household card [10-K line 00157]. Costco's warehouse operations feature strict control over entrances and exits to minimize inventory losses (shrinkage) [10-K line 00139], and it typically carries fewer than 4,000 SKUs per warehouse in its core business, significantly less than other broadline retailers [10-K line 00141].

Costco also offers various ancillary services such as gasoline stations, pharmacy, food courts, optical services, and tire installation [10-K line 00148], while engaging in e-commerce, business centers, travel, and other businesses that complement its warehouse operations [10-K line 00153].

# Market Positioning and Growth Outlook
Costco competes with global, national, regional wholesalers and retailers including supermarkets, supercenters, online retailers, gasoline stations, hard discounters, department and specialty stores, and operators selling narrow ranges of merchandise [10-K line 00190]. In the U.S., significant competitors include Walmart, Target, Kroger, and Amazon [10-K line 00190], with other warehouse clubs such as Sam’s Club and BJ’s Wholesale Club also competing in the U.S. [10-K line 00190].

The company's competitive advantages include its membership model, which reinforces member loyalty and provides continuing fee revenue [10-K line 00406], and differentiates itself through private-label products such as Kirkland Signature, which are high quality, offered at prices generally lower than national brands, and help lower costs while differentiating offerings [10-K line 00192]. Key catalysts include membership growth, particularly Executive memberships that earn rewards redeemable at warehouses [10-K line 00166], a high member renewal rate (92.3% in U.S. and Canada, 89.8% globally) that supports profitability [10-K line 00167], and the company's ability to leverage SG&A expenses through increased comparable sales [10-K line 00398].

In 2026, Costco reported a 10% increase in net sales to $297.2 billion, driven by comparable sales growth and sales from new warehouses [10-K line 00451]. Digitally-enabled sales have grown significantly, representing 11% of total net sales in 2026 and increasing by 21% during 2026 [10-K line 00452]. The company opened 28 new warehouses in 2026, including relocations, for a total of 25 net new warehouses [release line 00014], with plans to open up to 33 new warehouses in 2027, including five relocations [10-K line 00513].
